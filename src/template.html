How the clock works

Total returns, not share prices

A fund’s share price leaves out the income it pays out. Every return on this page is a total return: distributions and dividends reinvested for BND, VTI, VOO, VXUS and Nvidia, and interest for TIPS and T-bills.

Fees

All returns are after each fund’s yearly fee (its expense ratio), so fees are counted once, inside the returns.

Monthly returns

Everything starts from monthly total returns since BND's first full month, June 2007: market prices with every distribution reinvested for BND, VTI, VOO, VXUS and Nvidia (NVDA), 3-month Treasury bills with no fees, and the consumer price index for inflation.

Dollars at work

BND's net assets come from Vanguard's annual reports for each December since 2007 and from Vanguard's fund page, filled in month by month from shares outstanding and the market price.

Opportunity cost

For each month: dollars at work × (the alternative's return − BND's return). The clock adds up those monthly differences without compounding them, so it counts what was missed each month, not what the missed gains might have earned later.

Between data updates

The data refreshes automatically each weekday evening after the market closes. Between refreshes the counters move at a steady pace, the way a debt clock runs between Treasury reports.

What it leaves out

Hindsight comparisons ignore risk. US stocks fell 37% in 2008 while BND gained 6.9%, and people hold bonds for stability, income and diversification, not to beat stocks. Taxes, trading costs and personal timing also differ. This page is information, not investment advice.
